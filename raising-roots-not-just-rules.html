Raising Roots, Not Just Rules

By Hilda Grace Nalwanga · Jul 28, 2026

A mother once told me, with tears she was clearly trying to hold back, "I have so many rules in my house, and yet my son still doesn't listen to me." I asked her a question back: "Does he know why the rules exist?" She paused for a long time before answering, "I've never actually told him."

We often build parenting around rules — bedtimes, screen limits, chores, curfews — because rules are visible, measurable, and easy to enforce in the moment. But rules without roots rarely survive adolescence. A teenager who has only ever been told what to do, and never why, has nothing to hold onto the first time a rule is tested outside our sight.

Roots are different. Roots are the values, the sense of identity, and the quiet daily proof of love that a child carries with them even when we are not in the room. UNICEF and the WHO describe this as nurturing care — responsive, warm, consistent relationships that shape a child's brain development and emotional security long before they shape their behaviour.

Positive discipline, the approach we teach in our parent coaching sessions, is built on exactly this distinction. It is not permissiveness, and it is not control for its own sake. It asks two questions of every boundary we set: is this kind, and is this firm? A boundary can — and should — be both. "I love you, and the answer is still no" is a complete sentence. It does not need to be followed by shouting to be taken seriously.

I think of roots and rules like a tree in a storm. A tree with deep roots and flexible branches bends in the wind and stands again. A tree with no roots, however rigid its trunk, is the one you find lying in the road the next morning. Our children will face storms we cannot always predict — peer pressure, disappointment, digital pressures we never grew up with ourselves. Rules alone will not hold them through those storms. Roots will.

So here is what I would gently offer any parent reading this: before you add one more rule to your home this week, ask whether your child understands the root beneath it. Tell them the why, not just the what. Rules change with every season of childhood. Roots, once grown, stay with a person for life.
